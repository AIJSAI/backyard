# S-404 — photos and clips on a reply, so a wedding is one thread

The founder asked a question: *"Can people post replies with photos or videos? i.e.
wedding photos post your photos so it's a thread?"*

**Threads existed; replies were text-only.** `Comment` carried
`author / post / body / via_email` and `MediaAsset` had a foreign key to `Post` alone, so
a family could not put their own wedding photographs under the wedding post — they got
five separate posts instead of one thread. No story covered it (S-401/S-402 are about
posting media; S-503 is emailing photos to a pod).

## Why this was the session's riskiest change

Not because it is large, but because of *what it touches*: the audience guard
(T-MEDIA-1), the media purge (T-MEDIA-6) and the digest capability ceiling. Half-right in
any of them means a photograph that outlives the reply it belonged to, or one that reaches
a side of the family it was never addressed to — **and every one of those would look
perfectly correct to the person who posted the reply.**

## The shape

**Exactly one owner, enforced in the database.** `MediaAsset` hangs off exactly one of a
post or a comment, via a `CheckConstraint`. Not a convention: every reader and every purge
branches on which FK is set, so a row with *neither* would be unreachable and unpurgeable
bytes, and a row with *both* would have two audiences.

**One audience path, not two.** Reply media routes through `scoping.visible_comments`
rather than re-deriving audience from the post. That buys two things in one move: a comment
already inherits its post's audience there, and a comment's own `deleted_at` is already
applied there — so a soft-deleted or taken-down reply takes its photos out of every surface
with no separate rule to forget. Re-deriving would be the second path TM-2 forbids, and
the probe below shows exactly what it costs.

**One ingest path.** `ingest_photo` / `ingest_video` take either owner. A separate
reply-photo function would be a second place for the re-encode, the pixel ceiling and the
metadata strip (TM-9) to drift out of step.

**No new limits.** The reply route reuses the composer's `_read_photos` and
`_validate_videos`, so the 20-photo cap, per-photo byte cap, video size/duration caps and
decode ceiling are the same objects, not copies.

## Four purge paths, three of which were holes

| path | before |
|---|---|
| author deletes their reply | no purge existed |
| moderator takes down a reply | no purge existed (S-713 promised it for a post's photos) |
| author deletes the **post** | comments cascade away — the **files stayed on the volume forever**, unreachable and unpurgeable |
| **member is removed** | their replies live on *other people's* posts, so the post loop never reached them — S-702's revocation-completeness promise was only true of top-level content |

The purge sits in the **service** for comments, unlike the post path's view-level purge,
because `removal._delete_content` reaches comments through a bulk queryset and never
touches a view.

## Two more surfaces that would have silently under-delivered

- **The elder page.** The wedding case fails on the surface it was written for if her
  grandchildren's reply photographs are invisible. Rendered as `<img src>`, never wrapped
  in a link — `test_elder_wcag` asserts every href on that page is the elder feed itself
  (S-601), and adding the picture is exactly the change that would tempt someone to make
  it tappable. Both halves asserted.
- **The member export.** *"Leaving takes your data"* had excluded every picture a member
  put under someone else's post — which, for a family whose weddings live in threads, could
  be most of what they contributed.

The digest **email** deliberately stays text-only: a capability-token image URL lands in
every forwarded copy and every provider's image-proxy cache. Asserted, with a non-vacuity
check that the digest really rendered the post first.

## Proof the tests bite — six mutations, six failures

| mutation | test that failed |
|---|---|
| audience re-derived from the post instead of `visible_comments` | **the soft-delete test** — the drift TM-2 warns about, exactly |
| digest ceiling narrowed on `post__in` alone (the pre-S-404 shape) | the token-widening test |
| author delete stops purging | disk-purge test |
| takedown stops purging | takedown-purge test |
| removal stops purging reply media | S-702 completeness test |
| export back to posts only | export test |

Plus the database refusing an asset with two owners, and with none.

## Live repro — and two harness errors worth recording

The real wedding case, driven through a browser: Priya posts to **Mom's side only**, replies
with two photos; Sam replies with **one photo and no text at all**. Three reply photos
render in the thread. Dave, on the other side of the family, gets **404 on the post and 404
on every reply photo URL**.

Two of my own mistakes on the way, both of the kind that produce a confident wrong answer:

1. **Take one made the post yard-wide to *both* sides**, so the other side legitimately saw
   it. The `200` looked like an isolation leak and was correct behaviour — a false positive
   that could have sent me fixing nothing, and in the other direction would have hidden a
   real leak behind a passing check.
2. **`form[action*='comment']` also matches the delete-comment forms**, so the reply click
   hit the wrong button and Sam's reply never landed. Then, once it did land, I counted the
   images before the page had settled and briefly believed there was a rendering bug —
   the data said otherwise, and re-measuring confirmed all three render.

Fixed by scoping the post to one side, selecting on `form[action$="/comment/"]`, and
handling the TM-3 widen-confirmation hop that a yard-wide compose goes through.

## One thing the render exposed

A reply's `× Delete` was a **bordered red pill** — the loudest thing beneath somebody's
wedding photographs. It now reads as a quiet link, exactly as a post's does in the feed
("we ship rooms, not referees"). Found by looking at the page, not by a test.

## Out of scope, deliberately

**Attachments on emailed replies (S-502).** Someone replying to the digest from their
phone's mail app with three photos attached is a genuinely different problem — untrusted
input arriving over a webhook, with its own threat surface. Folding it in would hide a real
decision behind a checkbox. It remains a founder call.
