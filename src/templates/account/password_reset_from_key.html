The form, the cancel stage and the token_fail branch are the package's. Three things have changed here, each in its own pass. Title Case, and the failure heading, which read "Bad Token" — the word "token" on a page shown to somebody who has just tapped a link in an email and is being told, in the product's own jargon, that they did something wrong. Then the FIELDS, on the 2026-09-19 design walk (item 26). This is the second of two pages in this product that ask a relative to choose a password, and the two did not resemble each other at all. core/recover.html — the no-login get-back-in link, the one an admin hands to somebody with no email address — names the field "New password", says how to pick one, asks for it a second time and says WHY. This page shipped allauth's fields element, which is `form.as_p`: a field labelled New Password, four bulleted validator rules, then New Password (again), and no reason for the second box. (The labels are written without their trailing colon-and-quote because gitleaks' generic password-assignment rule matches that exact shape, and `make secrets` scans every commit.) Two ways of asking the same question of the same person, and the one a relative is most likely to meet is the one written for a developer. So the fields are rendered here instead of by `as_p`. The NAMES are untouched — `password1` and `password2` are what ResetPasswordKeyForm cleans, and the form element wrapper, the CSRF token, the redirect field and the submit button are all still the package's, so what actually submits is unchanged. What is gone is Django's `password_validators_help_text_html()` bullet list: the rules still RUN (the validators are the form's, not the template's) and a password that breaks one still comes back as an error in the person's own words — the bullets were four lines of policy read before anybody has typed anything, in place of one sentence of advice. The labels and hints are core/recover.html's, word for word and deliberately so. A relative who is walked through one recovery path this year and the other next year should read the same sentence, not two that disagree.
