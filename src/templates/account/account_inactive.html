Removal deactivates the account and leaves the password alone (core/removal.py step 3, and nothing in the revocation registry touches it), so a removed relative who types their old password still gets it right — and landed here, on the library's page: "Account Inactive" as the heading and "This account is inactive." as the entire explanation. That is a database state read aloud. It does not say what happened, it does not say a person decided it, and it gives somebody's grandmother a sentence about an account when what she wants to know is whether she did something wrong. NO NAME ON THIS PAGE. It is reachable by anyone who is not signed in — /accounts/inactive/ is a plain URL, and the sign-in form that redirects here answers to a guessed password as readily as to a real one — so it says "the person who invited you" rather than naming them. The person who removed somebody already knows who to talk to; a stranger who reaches this page learns nobody's name from it. No "Back to sign in" link, unlike the other entrance overrides. Those are for somebody who can still get in and needs a way back to the form; this reader cannot, and a link back to the thing that just refused them is an invitation to keep trying. The footer still offers How It Works and About underneath, which are the two pages that answer "what was this".
