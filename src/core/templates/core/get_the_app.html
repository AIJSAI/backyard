Get The App

{% comment %} "It is not in the App Store or Google Play" is the one place this is said, and it is an instruction, not a defence: a relative told "get the app" goes to the store and searches, finds nothing, and concludes it does not exist. {% endcomment %}

Add Backyard to your home screen. It opens like an app. It is not in the App Store or Google Play: the steps below are the whole install.

{% include "core/_install_steps.html" %} {% comment %} THE ONE THING THAT SURPRISES PEOPLE. An installed home-screen app on iOS has its own storage, separate from Safari's (webkit.org/blog/10218), so the session a relative has in Safari right now does not exist inside the icon they just made: they open it and see the sign-in page, which reads as "it did not work". An Android install usually shares the browser's, so the sentence says "may", and it is true on every phone that reads it. The second paragraph is the case this product creates. An admin-issued Sign-In Link (core/recovery.py) is handed over by text and opens wherever that phone opens links: a browser, or a mail app's own viewer, and on Android sometimes the installed app. So "usually", and "your browser", never "Safari". What the link does is set a password and then show the sign-in page WITH THE USERNAME FILLED IN (recovery_views.redeem): that is the only time this member is shown their username, they are by construction somebody with no e-mail on file, and the installed app opens with that box empty. The first cut told them to "sign in with that password" and left the username out, which walks them into the dead end the recovery flow was built to remove. {% endcomment %}

Signing In

The app may ask you to sign in again, once. On an iPhone it keeps its own sign-in, separate from Safari.

A Sign-In Link usually opens in your browser, not in the app. Use it to choose a password, and note the username on the next screen. Then open Backyard from your home screen and sign in with that username and password.
